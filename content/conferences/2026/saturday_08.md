<h1>The Effects of Proficiency on Incidental Learning of Words versus Multiword Expressions</h1>
<p class="abs-people"><strong>Mahnaz Aliyar</strong>, <span class="aff">Victoria University of Wellington</span></p>
<p class="abs-when">Saturday, September 19 · 18:25–18:50 JST · Session 4</p>
</div>
<div class="abs-body">
<h2>Abstract</h2>
<p>Vocabulary knowledge underpins second language (L2) proficiency, and a considerable portion of it develops incidentally through meaning-focused reading. Authentic comics, which include written text with visual imagery, offer a promising yet under-researched medium for such learning, providing rich contextual and pictorial cues that may scaffold the uptake of unfamiliar lexical items. A critical but unanswered question is whether learning of single words and multiword expressions (MWEs) benefit equally from these affordances, and whether any such difference depends on learner proficiency. MWEs, with their often semi-fixed and non-compositional nature, pose learning challenges distinct from those of single words, yet the interaction between lexical type, contextual support, and proficiency has received limited empirical attention in authentic multimodal contexts.</p>
<p>This study investigates how proficiency level shapes the incidental acquisition of single words versus MWEs from pleasure reading of an authentic English-language comic. Two groups of 40 Chinese learners of English, one advanced and one intermediate, each read the comic Kampung Boy: Yesterday and Today (Lat, 1993) once during their leisure time. The text features occasional Malay lexical items, 20 of which (11 single words, nine MWEs) served as target vocabulary and were treated as pseudowords. These items appeared under four naturally occurring conditions: pictorial support, in-text glossing, combined (multimodal) support, and no support. Uptake was assessed immediately and after a three-week delay through form recognition, meaning recall, and meaning recognition tests.</p>
<p>Grounded in dual coding and multimedia learning frameworks, and using mixed-effects modelling, the analysis compares how learner proficiency interacts with support type and lexical type across the three dimensions of vocabulary knowledge. The study seeks to advance theoretical understanding of how proficiency moderates the incidental learning of single words and MWEs from authentic multimodal input, and to inform the pedagogical use of comics, glossing, and imagery in L2 vocabulary instruction.</p>
