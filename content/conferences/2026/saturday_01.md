<h1>Research-based Lexical Targets in Education and Teaching Materials</h1>
<p class="abs-people"><strong>Batia Laufer</strong>, <span class="aff">University of Haifa</span></p>
<p class="abs-when">Saturday, September 19 · 14:30–14:55 JST · Session 1</p>
</div>
<div class="abs-body">
<h2>Abstract</h2>
<p>Non-native language users operate with significantly limited vocabularies compared to native speakers. This reality presents fundamental challenges for school education regarding the required lexical targets and their implementation in teaching materials.</p>
<p>Textbook design is particularly critical, as textbooks serve as the primary medium through which lexical items are introduced, practiced, and recycled. Ideally, they should align closely with the lexical targets established by educational systems. Yet research across multiple contexts reveals persistent problems: textbooks often lack clear vocabulary selection criteria, fail to match learners' proficiency levels, provide inadequate recycling of lexical items, and do not appear to be grounded in research-based principles (Antes, 2025; Bergström et al., 2025; Biseko, 2025; Brown, 2011; Konstantakis &amp; Alexiou, 2012; López-Jiménez, 2014; Sun &amp; Dang, 2020).</p>
<p>This presentation demonstrates how empirical research on vocabulary learning has informed the principled construction of a lexical syllabus for elementary and secondary education, subsequently implemented in school textbooks. The syllabus incorporates key research findings regarding: the amount of lexis necessary for performing language tasks; type of words necessary for such performance; distinctions between receptive and productive knowledge; relationships between proficiency levels and word counting units; L1 influence; multiple word meanings; the importance and acquisition of multi-word units. The implementation of the lexical syllabus in the textbooks has been informed by research findings regarding effects of repeated exposures, spacing, and recycling; and optimal types of vocabulary activities (Laufer, 2020; Nakata, 2015; Nation, 2015).</p>
<p>The result is a set of uniform, country-wide word lists spanning Grades 4-12 (CEFR Pre-A1 to B2), with all target words systematically integrated into level-appropriate textbooks through both texts and exercises. I examine the interface between research and policy-making, illustrating how accessible, sound research evidence can inform effective educational decision-making.</p>
